The watch, three ways

Four screens from your wrist: your week, the set, changing a number and the rest. Each direction draws all four so you can compare them side by side. Tap any watch to see it bigger; the big one is close to the real size of the face.

All three drop the set counts from the week, the same as the board. Nothing changes on the phone, and nothing ships until you pick.

Today watch 1.6

For comparison. Warm cards and ember text, but none of the phone's plates, cells or anvils.

A. Plates the board on your wrist

Every row and number sits on a blackened steel plate with the ember rail, exactly like the cards on your week. A finished day turns its rail gold and stamps an anvil. The set you are on is one big plate; the sets below are small plates coloured by grade.

B. Ledger the session screen on your wrist

A dark header band across the top holds the name, like each lift on your phone's session screen. Weight and reps are two separate cells, and the one the bezel is turning lights gold. Your sets below are ledger rows tinted by grade. The week uses the same day column as your board.

C. Seal made for a round face

Uses the circle instead of fighting it. The rim carries one arc per set of this lift, gold, violet, ember for the one you are on, steel for the rest, so a glance at the edge tells you where you are. Rows are round pills with a seal that stamps an anvil when a day is done, like your finish screen. The rest drains around the rim. One honest catch: watch 1.2 chose a straight bar for the rest because the edge of the glass is where a sleeve sits; this puts it back on the rim.

What building any of these involves

  • The watch gets the phone's two typefaces, Marcellus for names and the mono for numbers, in regular weight only.
  • The workout overview and the swap list follow the week's rows, and the plate calculator is not on the watch.
  • Only the look changes. What each screen does, and every button, stays where it is.
  • You can mix: say, the week from A with the set from B.
