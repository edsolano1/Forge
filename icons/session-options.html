The session screen, three directions

Same workout in each: Chest, Triceps and Core, two sets of the bench logged, one gold, one violet, a superset further down, the rest timer running. All three keep the anvil to log a set and the finish button at the end. They differ in how much you see at once and where the eye lands.

AA. Plates on the bench

The board's language carried straight in: each lift is a blackened plate with the ember rail, its sets are slots inside it, a logged set lights gold or violet along its whole row. Tools are small icons on the plate's shoulder. Supersets are lassoed in violet. Closest to what you have, made of iron.

AB. The ledger

Denser. Each lift is a compact table: a header line with the name, the rest and the tools, then one tight row per set with the weight and reps as two tappable cells and the anvil at the end. Cues sit behind the name on tap. Most of a workout fits one screen, so you scroll less mid-set.

AC. Focus

One lift at a time. The current lift is a big plate with the weight and reps large and one strike button; a row of pips shows the sets, gold and violet as they land. The rest of the workout is a spine underneath, done lifts dimmed with a gold stop, supersets marked. Most game-like, least scrolling, and the least like a spreadsheet.
