The ground, three directions

The same blackened steel board on three different grounds. Each keeps the ember palette; they differ in where the light comes from and what the ground is made of.

H. The hearth

Near black. One hearth sits low behind the board and its light rises, catching the bottom edges of the plates. A few embers drift up near it and nowhere else. The plates read as lit from below, the way iron does at a forge.

I. Soot and stone

The quietest. A near black ground with a fine grain, like soot on stone, a cold edge of light along the top and only a trace of hearth at the foot. The plates and the ember rails do all the talking. Heaviest by contrast, calmest by mood.

J. Hammered iron

The ground is iron itself: a brushed diagonal grain over near black, the hearth off to the lower left so light falls across the board at an angle, and a faint anvil standing in the dark behind the plates. The most workshop of the three.
